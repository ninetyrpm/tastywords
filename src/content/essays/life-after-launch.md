I do not think the answer is to treat a stable marriage as a product in need of constant reinvention. Nor is it to introduce novelty simply to prove that the relationship is still alive. We have already changed together more times than either of us could have predicted, and the durability of what we have built is real.

But durability and stability does not mean that our work is finished. I cannot let everything I know about my wife determine what I am still capable of hearing from her. And I cannot expect her to know me if I reveal only the thoughts I have already resolved and the choices I have already made.

> The more a relationship remains open to who its people are becoming, the more uncertainty it must endure.

> The more a relationship depends on who they have already been, the more familiarity becomes finality.


A relationship can become familiar without becoming closed. It can be reliable without requiring either person to remain exactly as they have been. You do not need to constantly seek novelty in a relationship. You need to avoid confusing familiarity with finality.
